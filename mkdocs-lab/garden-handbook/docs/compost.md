# Compost

The heap lives behind the shed. It
takes most of what the garden grows
and gives back the mulch and soil
improver the plots run on.

## What goes on the heap

**Greens** (wet, nitrogen-rich):
grass clippings, vegetable peelings,
spent annual plants, tea leaves and
coffee grounds.

**Browns** (dry, carbon-rich):
shredded cardboard, straw, fallen
leaves, woody prunings cut small.

Aim for roughly as much brown as
green by volume. All green and the
heap turns slimy; all brown and
nothing happens until next year.

## What never goes on the heap

!!! danger "Keep these out"
    - Cooked food, meat, dairy — they
      draw rats.
    - Dog and cat waste.
    - Perennial weed roots (bindweed,
      couch grass) and anything gone
      to seed, unless the heap is
      running hot enough to steam on
      a cold morning.
    - Glossy paper and treated timber.

## Turning

The heap is turned at the spring and
autumn working parties. Fork the
outside to the middle, the middle to
the outside, water it if it is dry,
and cover it again. Finished compost
is the dark, crumbly layer at the
bottom of the oldest bay — take it
from there, not from the top.
